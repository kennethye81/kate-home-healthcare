# Documentation Burden, Scheduling Friction Fuel Home-Based Care Turnover

**Source:** Home Health Care News (HHCN)
**Author:** Morgan Gonzales
**Date:** July 2026
**Key Themes:** Workforce turnover, retention, administrative burden, scheduling

A report from Homecare Homebase found that 63.3% of home health providers reported turning down referrals. Referral conversion rates have declined 13%, while referral rejections tied to staffing shortages remain about twice as high as pre-COVID-19 pandemic levels.

**Key data points:**
- Home health RN turnover: 25.46%
- Home health aide turnover: 34%
- Personal care provider/caregiver turnover: exceeds 70% annually in many markets
- Early-career turnover is disproportionately concentrated among first-year clinicians
- Nearly half of nurses with fewer than 10 years of experience report burnout

**Core finding:** "Retention is often discussed as a staffing issue. In practice, it is an operational issue with workforce consequences."

**Five areas clinicians identify for sustainable work experience:**
1. More time with patients
2. Less after-hours work
3. More predictable schedules
4. Clearer visibility into workload expectations
5. Tools that reduce administrative burden

**Caregiver priorities:** Competitive compensation remains foundational, but caregivers also want consistent schedules, reliable hours, simpler administrative processes, stronger communication, and greater day-to-day stability.

**Implication for providers:** Retention becomes a capacity strategy — organizations risk losing clinicians faster than the workforce pipeline can replace them.
