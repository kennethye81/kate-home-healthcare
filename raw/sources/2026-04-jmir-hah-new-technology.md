# Hospital-at-Home: New Technology Brings Acute Care to Patients' Homes

**Author:** Jenna Congdon, JMIR Correspondent
**Journal:** Journal of Medical Internet Research (JMIR), 2026;28:e98143
**PMCID:** PMC13094887 | Published: 2026-04-20

## Key Takeaways
- HaH programs combine remote monitoring, wearables, AI, telehealth with in-person care
- Key barriers: payor policy change, data security, interoperability, scaling digital infrastructure
- Improves patient satisfaction, lowers costs and readmissions, reduces hospital complications

## History
- Care at home was norm before modern medicine (19th-20th century centralization)
- 1994: Bruce Leff (Johns Hopkins) pioneered HaH with pager + bulky portable phone in backpack
- Proved: costs ↓, LOS ↓, delirium ↓; patients/caregivers reported less stress

## Core Components of HaH
1. Remote physiologic monitoring (wearable sensors)
2. Digital communication + telehealth visits
3. Frequent in-person visits + 24/7 response
4. Multidisciplinary care (physicians, nurses, pharmacists, therapists)
5. Home-based interventions (medication, oxygen, imaging, lab, therapy)
6. Robust logistics (transport, meal delivery, care coordination)

## Key Quote
Bruce Leff: "The hospital of the future is ER, OR, and ICUs... Most of the other stuff will move out."
