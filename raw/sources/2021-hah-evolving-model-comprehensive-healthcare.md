# Hospital at Home: An Evolving Model for Comprehensive Healthcare

**Authors:** Henil Y. Patel, Daniel J. West
**Journal:** Global Journal on Quality and Safety in Healthcare, 2021;4(4):141
**PMCID:** PMC10229033

## Abstract
Hospital at Home (HaH) is a sustainable, innovative, and next-generation model of healthcare. This model provides cost benefits and quality improvement, and helps in providing patient-centered medical care. The HaH model was first introduced at Johns Hopkins in the US in 1995, with promising results in length of stay, readmission rates, patient satisfaction, and hospital-acquired infections. The model provides acute critical care to patients at home and reduces unnecessary hospitalization. Identified patients are elderly with chronic conditions and multiple comorbidities.

## Key Data Points
- Johns Hopkins HaH: total cost 32% less than hospital ($5,081 vs $7,480)
- Mean LOS shorter by one-third (3.2 days vs 4.9 days)
- Delirium occurrence lower (9% vs 24%)
- Presbyterian Healthcare Services: falls 0% vs 0.8%; 30-day readmission 10.8% vs 10.5%
- Mortality: 0.93% HaH vs 3.4% comparison
- Mean costs 19% lower
- Mount Sinai (n=507): readmission 8.6% vs 15.6%; ED revisits 5.8% vs 11.7%
- Skilled nursing admissions: 1.7% vs 10.4%; LOS 3.2 vs 5.5 days
