# Asia-Pacific Front Line of Healthcare 2026

**Authors:** Vikram Kapur, Alex Boulton, Lucy d'Arville, Dhruv Sukhrani, Monica Pinto Basto
**Firm:** Bain & Company (4th biennial report)
**Survey:** 600 doctors (Australia + Philippines) + 6,300 consumers across 9 Asia-Pacific markets

## At a Glance
- APAC patients have become consumers: more demanding, informed, seeking preventive care, spending more
- 1 in 5 doctors actively considering switching organisations
- AI capabilities developing faster than organisations can adopt

## Persistent Pain Points
- APAC: 60% of world population, 22% of global healthcare spending
- Emerging APAC: 1.6 doctors/1,000 people (0.9 excluding China) vs WHO min 2.5, OECD avg 3.7
- Long wait times remain #1 frustration (consistent since 2019)
- <70% of chronic patients have regular checkups (cost is #1 barrier)
- 30% of doctors believe recruitment/retention harder since 2023
- 20% actively considering switching (driven by workload, not compensation)

## Key Trends
1. **Consumerism**: 84% expect more convenience; 71% expect more responsive doctors; 75% more informed than ever
2. **Preventive care surge**: 60% scheduling regular checkups (2025) vs 47% (2023); China leads at 76%
3. **Care everywhere**: 57% use alternative care sites; home-based care leads in India/Vietnam; telehealth leads in China/Singapore
4. **Telehealth**: Doubled 2019-2021 then moderated; China outlier at 61% penetration; India collapsed to 10%
5. **Fragmentation**: 50% sent to multiple providers before correct diagnosis; 40% received inconsistent advice; 55% of chronic patients see multiple doctors
6. **AI acceptance**: ~75% comfortable with ≥1 AI healthcare app; higher than US; strongest for clinician-support use cases

## 5 Strategic Opportunities
1. **Become a trusted coordination point** — 95% want single touchpoint; primary care underinvested; alternative coordinators (pharmacy, digital, payer) emerging
2. **Redesign care journeys around moments of truth** — billing/coverage = strongest detractor creator; promoters 2.5x more likely to stay, 2x more likely to expand use
3. **Implement value-based care** — 56% of doctors more interested in VBC than 2 years ago; momentum building (DRG in China/India, Healthier SG); bottleneck is operational backbone
4. **Treat AI as business transformation** — 95% of leaders believe AI will transform revenues/costs; >50% of early deployers report meaningful ROI within 12 months; Apollo 1,300-condition CDSS, SGH PEACH (98% accuracy, 660 hrs/yr saved), Ping An Good Doctor 4M consultations/day
5. **Elevate clinician engagement** — Higher nurse burnout → higher mortality; engaged doctors have eNPS 36 points higher; foundational enablers (tech, workflows, staffing) frequently fall short

## Implications by Stakeholder
- **Payers**: Stop administering claims → start owning care. Redesign billing (biggest detractor), move to VBC, deploy AI
- **Providers**: The relationship is the asset. Build outpatient ecosystem, fix clinician experience, embed AI with clinician buy-in
- **Pharmacies**: Shift from dispensing → coordinated care platforms. Become default entry point for routine care
